The most repeated take on my timeline this month: AI tools widen the gap between senior and junior engineers. I think that is right, but most of the conversation stops at the diagnosis. The harder question is what onboarding looks like when the new-hire default is an AI-assisted first draft they cannot evaluate.

## Outline

- The mechanism, stated plainly: seniors recognize wrongness shape because they have the system model; juniors cannot
- Why the usual fix ("just turn off Copilot for juniors") does not work and is not going to happen anyway
- A better frame: AI as tutor, not author
- Concrete onboarding patterns that survive AI assistance:
  - Code-review-as-curriculum — reviews are the teaching artifact, not a gate
  - Socratic mentor loops — questions back at the junior, not answers
  - Short, well-scoped first issues with an explicit expected-failure list
  - "Explain the diff in your own words" as a required PR section
- A platform-eng angle: onboarding is a product, and AI pressure is a forcing function to treat it that way
- How FireFly's Socratic tutor shape transfers directly to engineering onboarding
- What does not work: pairing that amounts to a senior rewriting a junior's AI output
